## Summary {#summary}

A 200+ product coffee catalogue serving households and businesses from one site. Split
navigation, a preparation-method finder, eight sub-brands on a shared attribute model.
Bilingual, live.

## Subtitle {#subtitle}

A 200+ product coffee catalogue reconciling B2C e-commerce and B2B corporate needs on a
single coherent site, with a bold visual system and purposeful motion. Bilingual, live.

## The challenge {#problem}

Spetema's audience is genuinely dual-natured. **Consumers** browse discovery-led, where
visual appeal converts. **Business and horeca buyers**, café owners and procurement teams,
move task-led, where clarity converts. Optimise purely for one and you frustrate the other.

## Information architecture {#approach}

The existing site had grown reactively for years: 200+ products spread across overlapping
categories, the B2B offer with no clear entry point, corporate information buried. I split
the top level into two tracks, a home track and a business track, so both audiences orient
within seconds instead of filtering past each other's content.

Underneath, a preparation-method finder layers over the category taxonomy. Category trees are
organised the way a business classifies coffee; a lot of people shop by the machine sitting
on their counter. The finder lets them enter that way without the taxonomy being rebuilt
around it.

Eight sub-brands are structured on a shared attribute model, so product information is
authored once and surfaced everywhere it's needed: consumer detail pages, B2B catalogue,
homepage features, without duplication.

## Visual system {#system}

- **Strong typographic hierarchy.** Large, confident headlines with clear reading order.
- **Product-first imagery.** UI frames the photography rather than competing with it.
- **Modular components.** The same card renders a consumer product, a business service, or an
  article.
- **Purposeful motion.** Every animation reviewed against one question: does this help the
  user understand what's interactive? Decoration was removed.

## Validation {#approach}

A/B variants for navigation wording, hero layout (product-first versus brand-story-first),
B2B entry prominence, and grid density, evaluated with stakeholders and lightweight user
testing. Winners were the approaches that worked for both audiences, not just one.

## Status {#status}

Live and bilingual. Handoff used annotated Figma components with motion specs: everything the
team needed to implement the system faithfully, without back-and-forth.
